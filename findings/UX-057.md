# The toolbars come back on any small scroll up, taking screen and moving the page

Open · medium severity · failure · Firefox on iPad

## What we saw

Scrolled down, Firefox shows only a thin address line and the page gets almost the whole screen (first screenshot: the newsroom header starts about 90 px down a 1,640 px screen). Any small scroll up restores the tab strip and full address bar (second screenshot: the header starts about 270 px down), about 180 px or 11% of the screen, and the page content shifts down by that much while it is being read. The hidden state is the one wanted all the time.

![Firefox on iPad scrolled down: only a thin address line above the newsroom header, the release history filling the screen](/findings/img/UX-057-toolbar-hidden.webp)

_Scrolled down: toolbars collapsed to a thin address line. This is the state wanted all the time._

![The same page after a small scroll up, with the tab strip and address bar back](/findings/img/UX-056-keyboard-after-scroll-up.webp)

_After a small scroll up: tab strip and address bar back, the page pushed down about 180 px (and, here, the keyboard too: UX-056)._

## Why it matters

The reader scrolls up a line to re-read a sentence and the whole page jumps, the text moves away from their eyes, and a toolbar they did not ask for takes a ninth of the screen. Each time, attention goes to the chrome instead of the text.

## The fix

For the browser: keep the toolbars hidden until a deliberate gesture (a tap on the address line, or a scroll to the very top), and offer a setting to keep them collapsed. For the sites, on our side of the line: keep our own sticky header as small as it can be, because it stacks on top of the browser's (on this page the newsroom's header takes about another 110 px).
