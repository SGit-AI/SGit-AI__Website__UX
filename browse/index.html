By principle

The rule it breaks. Each principle has a one-minute test on the principles page.

PrincipleFindingsOpen
A decision comes with what you need to make itAny prompt that asks the reader to decide (allow, pay, share, delete) first says, in plain words, what will happen, what it touches, whether it can be undone, and why it is being asked. The raw detail is there for checking, not as the explanation.11
The first screen is for a strangerOn a phone, the first screen says what this is, who it is for, and where to go next. The lead is on it. Chrome, metadata and method come after.86
Nothing moves that the reader did not moveWhile the reader is reading, the interface holds still. Toolbars, keyboards, banners and panels appear, move or take focus only on a deliberate gesture made for that purpose, never as a side effect of scrolling or reading.32
The same gesture gives the same result, so it can be learnedEvery interaction behaves the same way every time, triggered by something the reader controls (where they tap, how far they scroll), never by something they cannot see or repeat (how fast they flicked, what state an animation was in).11
Label the money, and never surprise with a chargeEvery number that is money says what it is. The first time anything is charged, the reader is told before or as it happens. Wording about balances never reads as debt or threat.54
A default is not an answerInputs that ask the reader's opinion start empty. Only what the reader actually did is recorded.22
Ask before keepingNothing is added to the reader's lists, profile or storage because they opened something. Keeping is a tap the reader makes.22
Every view is a linkAnything a reader can see, they can send: a filter, a search, a tag, a desk, a part of a series. The address changes when the view does.43
No dead endsEverything that looks like a way in is one: tags link to pages, missing pages offer the way back, each article ends with one next step.76
One source of truth for every factEach piece of content is published once, at one canonical address. Every count, date and label is generated from one manifest at build time.43
The publication is not the workshopReader pages carry reader things. How the site is built (versions, author tooling, agent strips, release history, admin) lives in one clearly separate area, one link away.44
Feedback reaches someone, and the reader gets a receiptEvery rating, request or 'what is missing' goes somewhere a person reads it, by default and with plain consent, and the reader keeps a receipt of what was sent.33
Say how long, and what kind, before the reader commitsEvery card and every link to a long piece shows reading time and format. Every long piece opens with a short version.33
Say who wrote it, and howThe byline names the person and the way the piece was made (with agents, from voice notes), where the reader looks, not in the footer. The purpose of the publication is one link away.33
Content is visible without waiting for scriptsThe page renders its content in HTML and shows it at once. Scripts enhance; they never gate what the reader can see.22
What a page says about the present is true nowLabels like 'edition of', 'currently', 'broken' and 'live' are dated or generated, and change when the facts do. A fixed problem is marked fixed, with the version.43
A vault app works inside the host, not just on its ownAn app is tested where readers open it: inside the vault host's sandboxed frame. Routing never assigns location.hash, storage goes through the host, and network reads come from the vault.33
